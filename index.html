About

AI has made building products faster, and put more of the software development lifecycle into a product manager's hands. What holds my attention is answering the new questions that a massive speed and efficiency boost opens up. 

Efficiency is valuable, but it isn't everything. Where should we intentionally introduce friction so people can exercise creativity, taste, and judgment, and what we make doesn't drift to the average? What's the right balance between accumulating context and expiring stale data, so that the product and business isn't optimizing for past successes? What needs to be true in AI-native product discovery and validation so that we make something distinctive, versus a confident imitation?

I've been working on questions like these, at the intersection of technology and creativity, during my whole career. The first code I wrote was for Digital Spy, where I wanted to foster editorial creativity and a vibrant user community. At Hearst I scaled that technology into MediaOS, the platform behind global brands like Cosmopolitan that are rightly legendary for their provocative writing. The technology changes, but what I build for doesn't — human creativity and judgment, amplified rather than replaced.

I'm drawn to products where AI meets content, media, and community. If you're building one, I'd like to hear from you.

Outside work, I bike, hike, travel, and enjoy music from the 80s. Born in London, New York City is home — I live here with my husband.
